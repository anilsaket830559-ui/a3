How to Read a Syllabus

A syllabus is the contract, map and calendar for a course. Ten careful minutes with it in week one can save hours of confusion later.

  • 5 units
  • About 8 minutes
  • Reviewed November 2026

Unit 1

Start with the learning outcomes

Outcomes describe what you should be able to do by the end of the course, such as analyse a source or solve a type of problem. Assignments and exams are written to test them, so they make an excellent checklist when revising.

Unit 2

Find out how you are graded

The assessment section shows how much each task counts.

  • Note the weight of every assignment and exam.
  • Look for minimum pass requirements on individual components.
  • Find the rubric or marking criteria if one is linked.

Unit 3

Copy every date into your calendar

Put each deadline, test and exam into one calendar on the first day, with a reminder a week before. Mark reading weeks and holidays too, so you can see your busiest weeks at a glance.

Unit 4

Read the policies before you need them

Late penalties, extension rules, attendance requirements and academic integrity policies matter most when something goes wrong. Knowing them in advance means you can ask for help early instead of after a deadline has passed.

Unit 5

Note who to ask, and when

Record your instructor's office hours and preferred contact method. Many questions are answered by the syllabus itself, so check it first, then ask, quoting the section you read.
